# CASE//FILE — Case Design Document
## CASE #017: "The 11:42 Window"

---

## A. CASE PREMISE

At **11:42 PM**, NEHA KOHLI — a 29-year-old senior financial analyst — disappears from her fourth-floor apartment. Her phone goes dark at 11:49 PM. The door is locked from the inside. There is no body, no blood, no sign of struggle that anyone can *agree on*.

The player is brought in as an independent investigator after the police stall on the case for lack of a body and conflicting witness accounts.

What looks like an abduction is not an abduction.
What looks like the timeline is not the timeline.
And the person everyone will suspect first is the only one who was actually trying to save her.

---

## B. FULL STORY (GROUND TRUTH — NOT SHOWN TO PLAYER UNTIL THE END)

Neha works at **Vantage Clear**, a fast-growing fintech startup, as the analyst responsible for reconciling vendor payments. Three weeks before the disappearance, she notices a pattern: recurring payments to a vendor called "Corebridge Logistics" that doesn't appear to do any real work for the company — the invoices are for "data infrastructure consulting" but there's no corresponding infrastructure. Over 14 months, roughly ₹40 crore has moved through this vendor and out again.

She quietly starts pulling records. She doesn't tell her boyfriend, Rahul, the full story — only that "something's off at work." She does tell her downstairs neighbor and closest friend, Tara, who is a lawyer, because she wants a second opinion on whether what she's seeing is actually fraud or just messy bookkeeping.

What Neha doesn't know yet: the CFO, Priya Desai, is aware of Corebridge and has been quietly signing off on it — not because she designed the scheme, but because the CEO, Arjun Mehra, told her it was a "structuring arrangement to manage investor optics" and she chose not to ask more questions, because Vantage Clear is about to close a funding round that would make her shares worth a great deal.

Ten days before the disappearance, Neha requests raw vendor banking records from IT "for a reconciliation project." That request is what tips Arjun off. He doesn't know exactly what she's found, but he knows it's enough to be dangerous with the funding round three weeks away.

Arjun does not plan to hurt her. His plan is to have his head of corporate security, Vikram Nair, quietly search her apartment while she's known to be out at a work dinner, to find out what she has and remove or copy it. Vikram has done "soft" jobs like this before — checking on a leaking employee, retrieving a company laptop. He is not a violent man, and he believes this is the same kind of job.

But Neha never went to the dinner. She canceled that morning, telling colleagues she wasn't feeling well. She had actually spent the last four days finalizing something else entirely: she and Tara had decided that going to the police wasn't safe yet — they had no proof of *threat*, only financial irregularities that a good lawyer (funded by Arjun) could spend two years explaining away. Neha wanted to first get the raw data into the hands of a financial journalist she'd been quietly corresponding with, and she was afraid that if Vantage Clear realized what she had, they'd either bury it through legal pressure or make it disappear along with her hard drive.

So Neha and Tara built a plan that, in hindsight, is almost absurdly simple:

1. Neha would leave the apartment quietly, early — around **10:55 PM** — through the building's rear stairwell (which has a broken camera she'd noticed weeks earlier while charging her phone in the stairwell during a power cut).
2. She'd leave her personal phone behind, powered on, in her apartment.
3. Tara, from her own apartment two floors down, would use Neha's laptop (already logged into Neha's phone-mirroring app for "sending photos to a bigger screen," a habit Neha's friends knew about) to send two outgoing texts and a fake "read" receipt on a conversation at **11:42 PM**, making it look like Neha was still home, alive, and awake at that time.
4. Rahul, Neha's boyfriend, would be Neha's alibi-support: he'd meet Neha two blocks away in his car at 11:05 PM and drive her to Tara's cousin's flat across the city, where she'd stay off-grid for a few days while the journalist verified the documents.
5. At 11:49 PM, Tara would power off Neha's phone remotely (using Find My Device) to end the fabricated activity window and make the "last known contact" time appear to be 11:49 PM — nearly an hour after Neha had actually left the building.

The plan worked almost perfectly, except for one thing nobody anticipated: **Vikram arrived early.**

Vikram entered the building at 10:48 PM (using a spare fob Vantage Clear's facilities team keeps for "maintenance access," which Arjun had obtained under a pretext), reached Neha's floor by 10:53 PM, and let himself into her apartment using a bump key at approximately **10:56 PM** — roughly one minute after Neha had already left through the rear stairwell. He searched the apartment for about 20 minutes, found her laptop missing (she'd taken it) and a mostly-cleared desk, panicked, and — worried that his forced entry would be discovered and traced back to him — deliberately damaged the front door lock further on his way out to make the scene look like the apartment had been broken into by a third party, muddying his own tracks. He left at 11:19 PM, before the 11:42 PM "disappearance time" even occurs in the false timeline.

So: the apartment was really entered and searched — but an hour before the time everyone believes, by a man who found nothing and no one, and who is not responsible for Neha's disappearance at all. He is, however, guilty of breaking and entering, and of tampering with evidence to protect himself.

The building's night security guard, Sameer, has his own small secret: he let his girlfriend into the building through the front entrance at around 11:00 PM for an unrelated visit, and to avoid trouble with building management, he later fudged his own entry log to say he was on his rounds the whole time — which is why his account of "who came and went" that night is unreliable and looks evasive, even though it has nothing to do with Neha.

**The truth:** Neha was never abducted. She staged her own disappearance, with Tara and Rahul's help, to safely get proof of Arjun's fraud to a journalist without being intercepted first. Vikram really did break into her apartment on Arjun's orders — but an hour before she "vanished," and he found nothing. The 11:42 PM disappearance time is fabricated evidence, created by Neha's own team, layered accidentally on top of a real, unrelated intrusion that happened earlier the same night.

---

## C. THE VICTIM

**Neha Kohli**, 29 — Senior Financial Analyst at Vantage Clear.
Methodical, quiet, not naturally suspicious of others, which is why it takes her three weeks to act on what she finds. Recently started therapy for anxiety (a detail some players may misread as instability — it isn't relevant to her disappearance and is a deliberate false lead about her "state of mind"). Has one sibling (estranged, living abroad — a dead-end the player can chase). No history of running away or "disappearing" before. Financially stable. Not in debt. No known enemies apart from, as it turns out, her own employer.

---

## D–E. THE SUSPECTS AND THEIR MOTIVES

### 1. Rahul Sen — Boyfriend, 31, UX Designer
**Motive (apparent):** Jealousy / recent arguments about Neha "pulling away" and being secretive for weeks.
**What makes him look guilty:** He lied to police about his whereabouts between 10:45–11:30 PM, claiming he was home alone. Neighbors' Wi-Fi router logs show his phone left his apartment's network at 10:51 PM. He was seen by a food delivery driver two blocks from Neha's building at 11:10 PM. He was visibly anxious and inconsistent in his first interview.
**What makes him look innocent:** No history of aggression. Genuinely distressed. Provides fingerprints/DNA voluntarily. His financial records show no unusual activity.
**The truth:** He lied because he was driving Neha to safety and had promised her and Tara he wouldn't tell anyone, including police, until the documents were safely with the journalist. He is protecting her, not hiding a crime against her.

### 2. Tara Bhatt — Best friend / neighbor, 30, Corporate Lawyer
**Motive (apparent):** Unclear — but she has unsupervised access to Neha's apartment (she holds a spare key), technical means (she's tech-savvy), and gave a suspiciously rehearsed statement.
**What makes her look guilty:** Her building fob logs show her leaving her own apartment at 11:38 PM and returning at 11:53 PM — squarely inside the "disappearance window." She refuses to fully explain what she was doing during that period in her first interview. She's a lawyer, which the player may read as "knows how to cover tracks."
**What makes her look innocent:** She has no financial or romantic motive. She's the one who eventually points investigators toward Vantage Clear at all (subtly, through a comment she "shouldn't" have made).
**The truth:** She left her apartment at 11:38 PM specifically to use Neha's laptop and phone-mirroring session to send the fabricated 11:42 PM messages and manage the fake "online" window, then returned once it was done. She was executing the plan, not covering up a crime against Neha.

### 3. Vikram Nair — Head of Corporate Security at Vantage Clear, 42
**Motive (apparent):** Unknown at first — he has no personal connection to Neha at all, which is itself suspicious once he's linked to the scene.
**What makes him look guilty:** Building maintenance-fob logs (obtained via subpoena) put him inside the building from 10:48–11:19 PM — squarely overlapping the real intrusion. Forensics find fresh tool-marks on Neha's front door lock. He has no explanation for being in the building that he's willing to give voluntarily. He has a professional security background (alarming to a player: "he'd know how to make someone disappear").
**What makes him look innocent:** No known relationship to Neha. No apparent motive. His personal financials are unremarkable — he wasn't paid extra for this.
**The truth:** He broke in on Arjun's orders to search for what Neha had found, arrived and left before 11:42 PM, found nothing, and damaged the lock further on the way out to disguise his own point of entry. He is guilty of breaking and entering and evidence tampering — but not of Neha's disappearance, which (in the false timeline) hadn't even "happened" yet when he left.

### 4. Priya Desai — CFO at Vantage Clear, 45
**Motive (apparent):** Financial — she has the most to lose from a fraud investigation (her equity, her career), and she personally signed off on the suspicious Corebridge payments.
**What makes her look guilty:** Her signature is on every Corebridge invoice. She stonewalls the player's records requests. She had a tense, recorded phone call with Neha nine days before the disappearance in which she tells Neha to "let it go, you don't understand the whole picture."
**What makes her look innocent:** She's visibly shaken when interviewed about Neha specifically — she has no idea Neha is even connected to anything beyond an accounting disagreement, because Arjun kept her deliberately uninformed about Vikram's assignment.
**The truth:** She's guilty of financial complicity (turning a blind eye to fraud for personal gain) but has no knowledge of, or involvement in, what happened to Neha. She is a genuine dead end on the disappearance itself — a real culprit for a different, smaller crime.

### 5. Arjun Mehra — CEO of Vantage Clear, 38
**Motive (apparent, once uncovered):** Protecting a ₹40 crore fraud scheme ahead of a funding round.
**What makes him look guilty (once found):** The maintenance fob used to access the building the night of the disappearance is registered to a Vantage Clear facilities account that only Arjun had recently requisitioned "for an offsite audit." Company server logs show a bulk deletion of Corebridge-related files at 11:52 PM the same night — after Vikram reported back to him. Financial records show the Corebridge vendor account is ultimately traceable to a shell company Arjun controls.
**What makes him look like a plausible "clean" executive at first:** Publicly, he's the company's calm, likeable face — genuinely surprised and cooperative when police first contact Vantage Clear, because at that point he still believes Vikram's search was quiet and unconnected to any "disappearance."
**The truth:** He ordered the break-in to find and suppress Neha's evidence, is guilty of large-scale financial fraud and of ordering the intrusion and evidence tampering — but even he did not "make Neha disappear." He spent the days after her disappearance genuinely trying to figure out what happened to her, since as far as he knows, his plan to have Vikram quietly search her apartment succeeded without her ever being aware.

### 6. Sameer Joshi — Night Security Guard, 26
**Motive (apparent):** None obvious, but his access logs are inconsistent with witness accounts of who was in the building and when.
**What makes him look guilty:** His written log claims he was on rounds on the 2nd and 3rd floors from 10:40–11:20 PM, but a resident reports seeing him at the front desk the whole time. He becomes evasive and defensive when questioned.
**What makes him look innocent:** No connection whatsoever to Neha, Vantage Clear, or any of the other suspects.
**The truth:** He let his girlfriend into the building for an unauthorized visit around 11:00 PM and falsified his rounds log to avoid disciplinary action — a real but minor and unrelated deception that nonetheless corrupts the reliability of the one "objective" record (the guard log) the player is most likely to trust early on.

## F. CLUES (18 TOTAL)

Each clue below includes: **type**, **surface reading**, and **true significance**.

1. **Front Door Lock Damage Report** (Physical) — *Surface:* Forced entry, suggests violent abduction. *Truth:* Damage was made by Vikram on his way *out*, to disguise his real entry method (bump key), not evidence of a struggle.

2. **Neha's Phone Recovery Report** (Digital) — *Surface:* Phone found still in the apartment, seemingly abandoned mid-abduction. *Truth:* Deliberately left behind as part of the plan; its "activity" after 10:55 PM was staged remotely by Tara.

3. **Outgoing Text at 11:42 PM ("just about to sleep, ttyl")** (Digital) — *Surface:* Proves Neha was alive and home at 11:42 PM. *Truth:* Sent by Tara via phone-mirroring software, not by Neha — the linchpin of the false timeline.

4. **Phone-Mirroring App Login Record** (Digital, hidden/late-discoverable) — *Surface:* Minor technical footnote. *Truth:* Shows a second device (Tara's laptop) was actively mirroring Neha's phone from 11:36–11:50 PM — the single clue that unravels the entire false timeline once cross-referenced with #3.

5. **Rear Stairwell Camera Maintenance Log** (Location) — *Surface:* Camera coincidentally broken same week — feels like a cover-up detail. *Truth:* Genuinely broken weeks earlier; Neha simply knew about it and used it, which is why no footage of her actual exit exists.

6. **Wi-Fi Router Log, Rahul's Apartment** (Digital) — *Surface:* Confirms/denies Rahul's alibi. *Truth:* Shows his phone left his home network at 10:51 PM, directly contradicting his statement that he was home all night — the reason he initially looks most guilty.

7. **Delivery Driver Sighting Statement** (Human) — *Surface:* Places Rahul near the building at 11:10 PM — looks incriminating. *Truth:* Consistent with him arriving to pick Neha up two blocks away, not entering her building.

8. **Building Facilities Fob-Access Log** (Location) — *Surface:* An unnamed fob entered at 10:48 PM and left at 11:19 PM — mysterious intruder. *Truth:* Registered to Vikram via a Vantage Clear account requisitioned by Arjun — the clue that connects the workplace to the apartment.

9. **Vantage Clear Server Deletion Log** (Digital) — *Surface:* Files deleted around midnight — looks like panic after "the disappearance." *Truth:* Deleted at 11:52 PM, immediately after Vikram reports back to Arjun — proof Arjun was actively covering up, and a critical timestamp for reconstructing the *real* order of events.

10. **Corebridge Logistics Vendor Invoices (14 months)** (Physical/Digital) — *Surface:* Dry financial paperwork, easy to skim past. *Truth:* The actual motive engine of the whole case — ₹40 crore in fraudulent payments.

11. **Priya Desai's Signature on Invoices** (Physical) — *Surface:* Looks like she's the mastermind. *Truth:* She's complicit but not the architect — a deliberate near-miss to tempt the player into naming the wrong "financial" suspect.

12. **Recorded Phone Call, Priya to Neha (9 days prior)** (Digital) — *Surface:* "Let it go, you don't understand the whole picture" sounds like a threat. *Truth:* Priya is protecting her own equity, not warning Neha on Arjun's behalf — she didn't even know about Vikram's assignment.

13. **Neha's Browser History — Journalist Email Thread** (Digital) — *Surface:* Looks like she was planning to flee the country or "run away" from her life. *Truth:* She was coordinating a leak to a financial journalist, the actual purpose behind everything.

14. **Tara's Fob-Access Log (leaves apt 11:38 PM, returns 11:53 PM)** (Location) — *Surface:* Puts her in the disappearance window unaccounted for — very suspicious. *Truth:* She left to execute the phone-mirroring plan on Neha's laptop, timed to generate clue #3.

15. **Building Guard Log vs. Resident Statement (Sameer)** (Human, contradictory pair) — *Surface:* Guard's official log conflicts with a resident's sighting — feels like it must relate to the disappearance. *Truth:* Unrelated — Sameer falsified his log over an unauthorized visitor, a genuine but irrelevant deception.

16. **Neha's Therapy Intake Form (found in apartment)** (Physical) — *Surface:* Suggests instability, possible self-harm or voluntary disappearance due to mental health crisis — an emotionally loaded red herring. *Truth:* Ordinary, unrelated anxiety treatment; deliberately included to test whether the player conflates "seeking therapy" with "unstable/unreliable."

17. **Neha's Missing Laptop** (Physical, notably absent) — *Surface:* Could suggest a thief/abductor took it. *Truth:* She took it herself when she left — its absence is proof she left under her own power, not evidence of theft.

18. **"Last Seen" CCTV Still, Building Lobby, 9:40 PM** (Location) — *Surface:* Shows Neha entering the building for what everyone assumes is the last time before "11:42 PM." *Truth:* Correct and undoctored — she genuinely never used the front lobby again, which is exactly why nobody clocks her real exit via the rear stairwell (clue #5). It's real evidence that's misread only because of the false 11:42 PM anchor point.

## G. TIMELINE

### The timeline as investigators initially reconstruct it (FALSE):
```
09:40 PM  Neha seen entering building lobby (CCTV)
10:45 PM  Rahul claims he was home all night
10:51 PM  Rahul's phone leaves his home Wi-Fi (unexplained)
11:10 PM  Rahul sighted 2 blocks from Neha's building
11:38 PM  Tara leaves her apartment (unexplained)
11:42 PM  Neha sends "just about to sleep, ttyl" — DISAPPEARANCE
11:49 PM  Neha's phone goes offline
11:53 PM  Tara returns to her apartment
```

### The real timeline (TRUE — revealed at the end):
```
09:40 PM  Neha enters building (genuine, last front-door entry)
10:48 PM  Vikram enters building via facilities fob
10:51 PM  Rahul leaves home to begin driving toward pickup point
10:53 PM  Vikram reaches Neha's floor
10:55 PM  Neha exits via rear stairwell (broken camera) — the REAL disappearance
10:56 PM  Vikram enters Neha's apartment (1 min after she's gone)
11:05 PM  Rahul picks Neha up two blocks away
11:10 PM  Delivery driver sights Rahul's car near the building
11:19 PM  Vikram leaves, damages lock on way out
11:36 PM  Tara logs into phone-mirroring session on Neha's laptop
11:38 PM  Tara leaves her own apartment to retrieve/verify Neha's laptop setup
11:42 PM  FAKE outgoing text sent remotely by Tara ("ttyl")
11:49 PM  Tara remotely powers off Neha's phone
11:52 PM  Vikram reports to Arjun; Arjun orders server file deletion
11:53 PM  Tara returns to her own apartment
```

**The critical contradiction:** Vikram's fob-access log (10:48–11:19 PM) and the server deletion timestamp (11:52 PM) are both *earlier* than the assumed disappearance time of 11:42 PM makes sense for — meaning if you take the 11:42 PM anchor at face value, Vikram's presence looks like it happened *before* anything occurred, which most players will initially dismiss as "irrelevant, wrong night" or "unrelated to the case." It's only when clue #4 (mirroring app log) proves the 11:42 PM text was faked that Vikram's early timestamps snap into place as the *real* center of the case.

## H. RED HERRINGS

1. **Rahul's alibi lie → assumed jealous-boyfriend abduction.** Real reason: protecting the escape plan.
2. **Tara's unaccounted 15 minutes → assumed accomplice to harm.** Real reason: executing the escape plan (same plan, opposite meaning).
3. **Priya's signature + threatening-sounding call → assumed she's the fraud mastermind and possibly ordered the disappearance.** Real reason: she's guilty of something, just not this.
4. **Sameer's falsified guard log → assumed cover-up related to the case.** Real reason: unrelated personal deception.
5. **Neha's therapy records → assumed mental health crisis / voluntary breakdown-driven disappearance.** Real reason: irrelevant, included to test player bias.
6. **Missing laptop → assumed theft/robbery motive.** Real reason: she took it herself; it's proof of agency, not victimhood.
7. **Vikram's professional security background → assumed he's a hired "cleaner."** Real reason: he's an amateur at this specific task who panicked and improvised.

The A→B→C chain most players will build: *Rahul lied (A) → he was near the building at the disappearance time (B) → he must have taken her (C)*. Clue #4 (mirroring log) is the "D" that proves the entire 11:42 PM anchor is fake, which retroactively breaks the chain — Rahul wasn't "near the building at the disappearance time" at all, because the disappearance actually happened 47 minutes earlier while he was already driving to meet her.

---

## I. CONTRADICTIONS

- **Primary contradiction:** The outgoing text at 11:42 PM (Neha "sending" a message) directly contradicts the phone-mirroring login record showing a second device actively controlling her phone at that exact time. One of these is a lie. This is the contradiction that breaks the case open.
- **Secondary contradiction:** Vikram's fob log shows him in the building 10:48–11:19 PM, but the front lobby CCTV (clue #18) never shows him entering through the front — because he used the facilities-only side entrance, which most players won't think to cross-check until they're specifically looking for how he got in.
- **Tertiary contradiction:** Sameer's guard log claims continuous rounds during a window a resident says he was at the front desk — a contradiction that *feels* central but is a dead end, teaching the player that not every contradiction points at the main mystery.

---

## J. TWO MAJOR TWISTS

**Twist 1 — "The timeline is fake."**
Just as the player builds a confident theory (usually centered on Rahul, sometimes Vikram), clue #4 surfaces: the phone-mirroring login proves the 11:42 PM text wasn't sent by Neha. The entire foundation of "when" the disappearance happened collapses, and everything has to be re-timed.

**Twist 2 — "There was no abduction. She planned this."**
Once the real timeline is reconstructed, the player will likely assume Tara and Rahul conspired to fake evidence to protect themselves after something went wrong — a cover-up theory. The second twist is that nothing went wrong: Neha is safe, alive, and orchestrated the entire thing herself to escape a real corporate threat and get fraud evidence to a journalist. The "victim" was the one directing the operation the whole time. Vikram's break-in was real and dangerous — but by the time it happened, Neha was already gone.

---

## K. THE ACTUAL SOLUTION

- **What happened:** Neha discovered financial fraud at her workplace, feared (correctly) that the company would move against her once it noticed she was investigating, and staged a fake "disappearance" with her friend Tara and boyfriend Rahul to safely deliver evidence to a journalist while appearing to vanish at a time that would give her a head start and muddy any investigation.
- **Who was "responsible":** No one caused Neha harm. The person whose actions created real danger and who is criminally responsible for a break-in, evidence tampering, and orchestrating it is **Arjun Mehra** (with Vikram as his instrument). The scoring system should treat "Arjun Mehra" as the correct answer to "who is criminally responsible in this case," while also crediting a player who correctly identifies that Neha herself was never actually a victim of abduction.
- **Why:** To conceal a ₹40 crore vendor fraud scheme ahead of a funding round.
- **Evidence that proves it:** Clues #4, #8, #9, #10, #13 form the core proof chain.
- **Key contradiction:** Clue #3 vs. Clue #4 (the fake text vs. the mirroring login).

---

## L. PLAYER'S LIKELY FALSE THEORY

Most players will land on one of two incorrect theories:

**False Theory A (most common):** "Rahul abducted or harmed Neha out of jealousy, then faked a message from her phone to cover his tracks, and Tara is either his accomplice or covering for him out of loyalty."

**False Theory B (secondary):** "Vikram, hired muscle for the company, abducted Neha on Arjun's orders because she found the fraud, and the company has been managing the cover-up ever since — meaning Neha may be dead or held somewhere."

Both are understandable, evidence-supported, and wrong — Theory A misreads loyalty as guilt; Theory B correctly identifies the company's guilt but wrongly assumes Neha was a passive victim rather than the plan's author.

---

## M. CORRECT THEORY (Player must construct)

- **What happened:** Neha staged her own disappearance with Tara and Rahul's help to escape a company that was about to silence her over a fraud discovery, faking the 11:42 PM timestamp to buy time.
- **Who was responsible (criminally):** Arjun Mehra, who ordered the break-in and evidence destruction; Vikram Nair as his instrument (lesser, non-mastermind culpability); Priya Desai for financial complicity (separate, minor charge).
- **Why:** To protect a large-scale vendor fraud scheme before a funding round.
- **Key evidence:** The phone-mirroring login record (#4) breaking the fake timeline; the facilities fob log (#8) connecting Vantage Clear to the apartment; the server deletion timestamp (#9) proving a cover-up in progress.
- **The key contradiction:** Clue #3 (fake outgoing text) vs. Clue #4 (mirroring session proving someone else controlled the phone at that moment).
- **What happened at 11:42 PM specifically:** Nothing happened to Neha at 11:42 PM. She had already left the building 47 minutes earlier. The text sent at that time was a fabricated alibi generated remotely by Tara to create a false "last seen alive" anchor point.

## O. DIFFICULTY ANALYSIS

- **Core difficulty driver:** Two independently plausible but incompatible timelines exist in the evidence, and the player must notice that clue #3 and clue #4 cannot both be true, then must be willing to discard a comfortable, well-supported theory (Rahul or Vikram as abductor) once that contradiction surfaces.
- **No trivia, law, or forensic science required** — every deduction is achievable through comparing timestamps, statements, and financial records already provided.
- **Estimated difficulty for an average intelligent player:** Moderate-to-hard. Expect most players to correctly flag Rahul and/or Vikram as suspicious early (intended), but a smaller fraction to catch the mirroring-app/timestamp contradiction without a nudge. The UI should support this via a "conflicting evidence" flag rather than requiring the player to notice it entirely unaided.
- **Fairness check:** Every fact used in the solution is directly stated in an evidence card. Nothing is inferred from outside knowledge.

## Q. SCORING SYSTEM

**Total = weighted average across 6 categories:**

| Category | Weight | What's measured |
|---|---|---|
